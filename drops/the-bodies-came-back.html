The Bodies Came Back

Israel returned roughly 400 Palestinian bodies to Gaza — and the 91 identified so far expose the casualty list: fighting-age men who died on October 7th, a quarter of them eulogized as “martyrs” by Hamas itself, all counted as war dead. A follow-up to Hamas's Own Numbers.

OCTOBER 11, 2026 · 5 MIN READ

After the ceasefire, Israel returned roughly 400 Palestinian bodies to Gaza. The Gaza Health Ministry published their photographs and the names of those it could identify — 396 listed, 91 identified. An independent researcher, Eirik Schrøder, did what the world's press did not: he matched the names.

What he found is the sequel to a story we published earlier — Hamas's Own Numbers — and it is worse. The bodies that came back were overwhelmingly fighting-age men who died on October 7th, 2023. A quarter of them had been announced as “martyrs” by Hamas and Islamic Jihad themselves. And every one of them sits on the Health Ministry's list of war dead.

I. The return

The bodies came back as part of the ceasefire arrangements. The Health Ministry in Gaza — run by Hamas — posted photographs of the dead and, where it could, their names and ID numbers. Schrøder took the 91 identified names and cross-referenced them against three sources: the Health Ministry's own casualty lists, Hamas and Islamic Jihad “martyr” announcements, and the “Genocide in Gaza” tracking website.

The first thing the data says: all 91 identified are men between 18 and 60. Sixty-three of them — 69 percent — carry a Health Ministry date of death of October 7th, 2023. Seventy-six — 84 percent — died on or before November 16th, 2023. These are not the demographics of airstrikes on apartment buildings.

II. Who they are

At least 23 of the 91 — a full quarter — were publicly announced as “martyrs” by Hamas's Al-Qassam Brigades or Islamic Jihad's Al-Quds Brigades. Not alleged. Announced — by the terror groups themselves, in their own martyrdom notices. Men like these died where Hamas's fighters died on October 7th: inside Israel, attacking it.

Israel had no ground presence in Gaza on October 7th. It could not have collected bodies there. The bodies it held — and has now returned — are the bodies of men killed on its own territory, on the day of the massacre. The Health Ministry's date-of-death entries agree: October 7th, 2023.

And yet every one of the 91 appears on the Gaza Health Ministry's casualty list — the list the world's media cites as the war's civilian death toll.

III. The list

Here is the detail that breaks the list entirely. Fifty-four of the 91 — 59 percent — were already on Health Ministry lists published before 2026. Thirty-five were on lists published before the ceasefire. Twenty-six were still on the last Health Ministry list issued before the bodies were returned.

Think about what that means. These men were listed as dead — counted in the toll, cited by journalists, mourned by activists — while their bodies were in Israeli custody the entire time. Nobody in Gaza saw these bodies. Nobody buried them. They were registered as presumed dead, and the presumption was printed as fact.

The casualty list, in other words, does not record confirmed deaths. It records claims.

IV. The airstrikes that never happened

The final cross-reference is the most damning. At least 68 of the 91 — 75 percent — appear on the “Genocide in Gaza” website, each described as killed by Israeli airstrikes inside Gaza: formulaic entries about aircraft targeting gatherings and homes in Rafah, Khan Yunis, Jabalia, Nuseirat — “without prior notice or warning.”

These men died inside Israel on October 7th. There were no airstrikes on Rafah that killed them, because they were not in Rafah. The website's descriptions are boilerplate — the same sentences, swapped city names — applied by default to deaths the site never investigated. Schrøder's analysis suggests the site assigns “death by airstrike” formulaically to the overwhelming majority of its roughly 50,000 entries.

So the pipeline is complete: a Hamas fighter dies attacking Israel on October 7th. The Health Ministry lists him as a war casualty. A “genocide” tracker dresses the death in an airstrike narrative. The world's press cites the numbers. Nobody checks — until the bodies come home and the names match the martyr posters.

A note of honesty, as always: this is Schrøder's forensic matching — his cross-referencing of IDs, names, and martyr announcements — not an independent audit. The methodology is on the table and the underlying lists are public. That is more than can be said for the casualty figures it corrects.

The ledger

The Gaza Health Ministry's list was never a count of the dead. It was a count of claims — including claims about men whose bodies were never in Gaza at all, men its own government's armed wing had already eulogized as martyrs of the October 7th attack.

The bodies came back. And with them came the receipts.
