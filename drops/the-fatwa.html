The Fatwa

Al-Azhar is the oldest and most authoritative institution in Sunni Islam — a thousand years old, half a million students, the Vatican's dialogue partner. On October 7th it saluted the massacre “with absolute pride.” Eleven days later, it issued a religious ruling: there are no innocent Israelis.

OCTOBER 8, 2026 · 6 MIN READ

There is one institution in the Sunni Muslim world whose word carries the weight of a thousand years. When it speaks, a billion people listen. On October 7th, 2023 — while the massacre was still unfolding — it spoke.

It did not condemn. It saluted — “with absolute pride.”

I. The institution

Al-Azhar was founded in Cairo in 998 as an academy for the study of Islam. Nationalized by Gamal Abdel Nasser in 1961 and placed under the authority of the Egyptian state, it has served ever since as the granter of religious legitimacy to the policies of Egypt's changing regimes. Its scale is difficult to overstate: Al-Azhar University is among the largest in the world, with some half a million students — tens of thousands of them foreign — and branches across the globe. In Egypt it runs a school system of about two million pupils, roughly a tenth of the country's schoolchildren.

In the West, Al-Azhar wears the robes of moderation. Barack Obama praised its “proud tradition of tolerance” in his 2009 Cairo address. In 2019 its Grand Imam signed the Document on Human Fraternity with Pope Francis. It issues fatwas against joining the Muslim Brotherhood, fosters Muslim-Christian dialogue, and presents itself as the ideological spearhead of the struggle against Islamic extremism.

That is one half of the institution. The Grand Imam since 2010, Sheikh Ahmed el-Tayeb, embodies the other: his recurring message is that “every occupation will disappear sooner or later” — Israel's existence, in other words, is temporary and fated for destruction. He maintains open, regular contacts with Hamas leaders — Ismail Haniyeh publicly praised him in 2017, and in February 2019 al-Tayeb welcomed a senior Hamas delegation including Haniyeh and Saleh al-Arouri. For over a decade, Al-Azhar has granted religious and moral approval to the violent struggle against Israel.

II. The salute

On the day of the October 7th attack — the cold-blooded murder of some 1,400 Israelis and foreign nationals, the rape of women, the abduction of the elderly, men, women and children — Al-Azhar published its response. It stated that it “salutes with absolute pride the resistance efforts of the Palestinian people,” and sought to “strengthen the proud Palestinian people who have breathed spirit and faith into us and restored us to life.” Fallen Hamas fighters were praised as “great martyrs of Palestine.”

Four days later, al-Tayeb spoke by phone with Haniyeh, who sought Al-Azhar's backing. The Grand Imam replied: “Our hearts are with you.” After Friday prayers on October 13th and 27th, demonstrators marched out of the Al-Azhar campus chanting “Khaibar, Khaibar, Jews, the army of Mohammed will return.” Senior Al-Azhar clerics took to Facebook: the inspector general of its fatwa committees voiced support for the attack on the day itself; a faculty dean praised the attackers and longed openly for the liquidation of the Jews.

III. The fatwa

Then, on October 18th, came the ruling. Al-Azhar's fatwa declared that “Zionist civilians on occupied land are not [at all] worthy of the description of ‘civilians’” — they are occupiers, usurpers, deviants from the way of the prophets. In effect: there are no innocent Israelis, and it is permissible to kill them. Al-Qaeda celebrated the ruling.

Four days later Al-Azhar published another statement — in Arabic, English and Hebrew — titled “Occupation is the Source of All Evil,” posing questions to the conscience of Israelis about 75 years of “crimes,” free of any examination of the crimes of Hamas.

Note what the institution did not do. Al-Azhar had previously issued a fatwa forbidding the abduction of children and women, even permitting the death penalty for their kidnappers. It issued no such call for the Israeli children and women kidnapped by Hamas. And the day after its statement of support — October 8th — an Egyptian policeman shot dead three Israeli tourists in Alexandria. Al-Azhar said tourists should not be harmed, but would not explicitly condemn the murder.

IV. The meaning

A fatwa is not an opinion column. For hundreds of millions of Sunni Muslims, it is as close as it gets to knowing what God is understood to say. When the supreme religious authority of Sunni Islam rules that there are no innocent Israelis, it does not merely excuse October 7th. It pre-authorizes the next one.

That is the meaning of the endorsement, and it travels. It grants religious cover to every imam, teacher and preacher who cites Al-Azhar — and they do, across the Sunni world. It makes it nearly impossible for other Islamic bodies in Al-Azhar's orbit to condemn Hamas: the UAE, which has spent years promoting religious tolerance, watched its own Council of Muslim Scholars — established jointly with Al-Azhar — refuse to condemn the slaughter. The poison flows downward, too: a November 2023 analysis of Al-Azhar's own K-12 textbooks found them demonizing Jews as treacherous and scheming, denying any Jewish connection to Jerusalem, and encouraging “popular resistance” against Zionist forces.

And through all of it, the partnership with the West continues uninterrupted — the interfaith dialogue, the funding, the moderation brand. As one Israeli research institute put it, the institute that presented itself as a bastion in the struggle against extremism has become one of its sources.

The seal

Fringe preachers cheer massacres every day; that is what fringe preachers do. This was not a fringe preacher. This was the thousand-year-old seat of Sunni learning — the Vatican's dialogue partner, the university of half a million students, the institution Barack Obama held up as a proud tradition of tolerance — looking at the worst massacre of Jews since the Holocaust and saluting it with absolute pride.

Eleven days later it put the salute into law. That is what a fatwa is: applause, made permanent.
